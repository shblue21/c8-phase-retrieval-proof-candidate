\begin{abstract}
Can 26 squared absolute values of complex linear functionals distinguish
every vector in $\mathbb C^8$ up to a common phase? We give an explicit
Gaussian-integer construction and a self-contained candidate proof that
they can. A vector is represented by a polynomial in a specified
eight-dimensional space. Three missing coefficients make 26 positive
samples determine its squared modulus. To show that the polynomial is
then determined up to phase, we compare two possible solutions through
their average and difference. Equal moduli impose pointwise real
orthogonality. Successive coefficients reduce this condition to positive
sums of squares and, in the last case, a real symmetric two-by-two
matrix whose determinant is strictly negative. The claimed conclusion
is $m_{\mathbb C}(8)\le26$, with no assertion of optimality.
The identities and frame entries have passed local review and separate
exact symbolic checks; external independent expert review is pending.
\end{abstract}
